PROHIBITED ITEMS NOTICE. Pursuant to Temporary Residential Sale Review Rule 14.7, an applicant shall not offer controlled substances, regulated weapons, ammunition, fireworks, recalled infant articles, prescription products, tobacco products, alcohol, live animals, fuel containers with residue, hazardous chemicals, automotive airbags, government records, counterfeit goods, or property known to be stolen. Household goods may be offered only when they are lawfully owned by the applicant or a member of the applicant's household and are reasonably described in the inventory. The Office may treat an item as commercial stock when quantities, packaging, labels, invoices, repeated sales, or other circumstances suggest purchase for resale. No determination under this notice relieves a person of an obligation under any other local, state, or federal rule. Electrical items with damaged cords, exposed wiring, missing covers, overheating, or recalled parts shall not be displayed. Cribs, car seats, helmets, smoke alarms, medical devices, cosmetics, pesticides, mattresses, food, and children's items may be subject to restrictions that are not stated here. An exception for a household goods donation exchange may be available under Section 14.7(c)(3), subject to conditions not reproduced in this application. The applicant is solely responsible for deciding whether an item may be sold, transported, stored, or discarded. The Office may ask for photographs, proof of ownership, inspection access, or a written explanation before review. A license does not approve any item that is unlawful, unsafe, mislabeled, recalled, or otherwise prohibited. Goods not listed may be treated as omitted material information. The Office may refer a matter to another agency, decline to consider the application, or revoke a determination if an item raises a question under a rule not identified in this notice. The words in this notice are illustrative and are not a complete statement of applicable obligations. Applicants should consult all rules independently before holding a sale. Repeated household goods sales, coordinated neighborhood sales, sale advertising, and use of rented display equipment may be considered when the Office determines whether an activity is temporary and residential. Items that appear to be part of a business inventory, estate liquidation, retail promotion, construction operation, repair operation, storage operation, rental activity, auction, consignment activity, or recurring sale may require another process even if they are ordinary household goods. The Office may consider signs, price tags, display fixtures, online advertisements, cash registers, credit-card equipment, shipping materials, volume discounts, purchase records, and coordinated sellers when making that determination. An applicant may not avoid a restriction by separating related goods among tables, participants, addresses, or days. The applicant must remove any item when directed by an authorized official and must not replace a removed item with a similar item without written direction.
